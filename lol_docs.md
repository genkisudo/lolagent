# Analyst implications:

Side and pick order are now decoupled. "Blue side first pick" is no longer a single unit, and any framework you built on that assumption is broken.
A team's First Selection choice is a readable signal about its draft philosophy: do they value map geometry (jungle pathing, drake side, blast cone escapes, ward angles) or draft leverage (securing a specific champion)?
Choices should shift patch to patch. If a team's First Selection decision never changes across a split, that's either a strong conviction or a coaching blind spot — say which you think it is.
Watch for Fearless × First Selection interaction: late in a series, pick priority may matter more than side because the remaining pool is thin and contested.
2. Patch and systems fluency (2026 Season One)

Riot's yearly seasonal model means the January patch is a near-reset. 2026 opened with patch 26.1 (new numbering: 26.x) and a large systems overhaul. An analyst who is still using 2025 vocabulary will sound obviously stale.

2.1 What was removed
Atakhan — removed entirely from Summoner's Rift, along with Blood Roses and Petals. (Narratively covered by Zaahen killing Atakhan in the Twilight's End cinematic.)
Feats of Strength — removed. First Blood and First Turret return to granting +100g and +300g respectively.
Baron Nashor returns to a 20-minute spawn.

Riot's stated reason: objective overload / objective fatigue. Removing Atakhan's second form and the second grub set mid-2025 wasn't enough.

Why this matters for analysis: the entire 2025 analytical framework around Atakhan side selection, Blood Rose stacking, and Feats-driven tempo is now dead vocabulary. Objectives are much closer to their 2024 state. Anyone still explaining games through those lenses is telling on themselves.

2.2 What was added or changed
Role quests. Every role now has a quest, extending the jungle/support pattern. Progress comes from normal play — CS, kills, plates, turrets, objectives — and is reduced when you're in the wrong lane, explicitly to discourage lane swaps. Top laners receive a free long-cooldown Teleport alongside their two summoner spells.
Epic monster durability. Roughly +15% health, plus stronger armour and MR. Smite deals more damage (harder to steal with abilities). Dragon Slayer stacks reduce your damage to dragons. Dragons and Baron give more comeback XP.
Turret pushing. New plates on Tier 2 and Tier 3 turrets, plus Crystalline Overgrowth — a build-up on towers that, when struck, is consumed for a burst of turret damage. Intent: make splitpushing and sieging viable again.
Faelights — new vision mechanic / vision system upgrades.
Faster starts. First minions at 0:30 instead of 1:05; post-laning waves every 25s instead of 30s, with cannons replacing a melee minion in midgame waves.
Level 20 and a 7th item slot.
WASD movement (shipped in 25.24, December 2025) and ~10 new items plus returning reworked classics.
2.3 How to build patch fluency (the actual skill)

Reading patch notes is table stakes. The expertise is prediction:

Read the dev blog, not just the notes. Riot states intent; intent tells you what they'll iterate on next.
Trace the causal chain. Faster minion spawns → shorter time to first wave → different level-1 invade math → different jungle clear timings → different first-objective contest windows. Write the chain out; check each link against actual pro games.
Separate solo-queue signal from pro signal. Coordinated five-stacks convert systems like Crystalline Overgrowth far better than ladder players. High ladder win rate ≠ pro viability, and vice versa.
Track the lag. Regions adopt new systems at different speeds. The gap between LPL/LCK adoption and everyone else is itself a story.
Name your breakpoints. Item cost thresholds, level 20 timings, plate windows — analysis that cites a breakpoint is checkable; analysis that says "the meta favours scaling" is not.
3. Macro frameworks and game-state reading

You need a vocabulary precise enough to explain why a game turned, in 30 seconds, without hand-waving.

Core competencies:

Tempo and trade evaluation. What is a lost bot lane actually worth against a taken Rift Herald? Be able to price trades in gold, XP, map control, and reset timing — not vibes.
Wave states. Slow push, freeze, crash, bounce timing; how the 25-second midgame wave cadence changes reset windows and dive setups.
Vision and information. With the new vision systems, ward coverage analysis matters more. Where is the information asymmetry, and who is playing as if they have information they don't?
Objective sequencing. With Baron back at 20:00 and epics tankier, the classic "Baron power spike vs. drake soul race" framework is live again. Know the soul math and the current dragon slayer stacking maths.
Splitpush and siege. Newly relevant in 2026. Recognise when a team is drafting for a 1-3-1, and whether their draft can actually hold the two side lanes.
Death timers and closing. The single most common gap in mid-tier analysis is failing to identify the exact window a team had to end and didn't.
Counterfactuals. "They should have gone Baron" is only analysis if you can say what happens next in both branches.
4. Ecosystem and format expertise

Formats are part of the competitive product. If you get the structure wrong, everything downstream is wrong.

4.1 The 2026 league map

The Americas restructure was reversed. The LTA lasted one season (2025) and is gone.

LCK (Korea) — started Jan 14
LPL (China) — started Jan 14
LCP (Asia-Pacific) — started Jan 16
LEC (EMEA) — started Jan 17
CBLOL (South America: Brazil + LATAM South) — started Jan 17
LCS (North + Central America) — started Jan 24

Details worth knowing:

LCS and CBLOL returned as independent leagues with restored branding, after criticism of the LTA's complexity, compressed formats, blurred regional identity, and declining viewership.
LLA does not return as a standalone league; Latin American teams are integrated into LCS and CBLOL structures. LYON is a partner team in the LCS; Leviatán in the CBLOL.
Both run guest team slots for tier-2 (LCS: NACL or LRN; CBLOL: CD or LRS).
Both operate a three-split calendar with updated, simplified formats and their own end-of-year Finals events.
Cross-regional playoffs were removed.
4.2 International calendar, 2026
First Stand — March 16–22, São Paulo (Riot Games Arena). Eight teams: LCK ×2, LPL ×2, LEC ×1, LCS ×1, CBLOL ×1, LCP ×1. Every match Bo5, Fearless throughout. Placement feeds MSI seeding, including a Play-In bye for the winning region.
MSI — June 28 – July 12, Daejeon Convention Center II, South Korea. Eleven teams (two per region except CBLOL's one). All Bo5, Fearless. Hanwha Life Esports beat Bilibili Gaming 3–2 in the final — HLE's first MSI title, and the first time a host-region team has won MSI. Zeus was MVP.
Worlds 2026 — Play-Ins through Semifinals at the Credit Union of Texas Event Center, Allen, Texas; Final in New York.
Hall of Legends returns later than usual, ahead of Worlds, with a third inductee.
4.3 The business layer
From 2026, Riot removed regional split prize pools in Global Revenue Pool regions, redirecting funds to broader ecosystem support. CBLOL and LCP are unaffected due to different partnership models. International event prize pools (First Stand, MSI, Worlds) remain GRP-funded.
Analysts should be able to discuss what this means for player earnings, tier-2 viability, and org economics without either parroting Riot's framing or reflexively dismissing it.
Adjacent knowledge worth having: slot sales and org exits, sponsorship volatility, salary structures, buyout mechanics, and the standard contract calendar that governs roster-move season.
5. Data and analytics competence

You do not need to be a data scientist. You do need to not embarrass yourself with numbers.

Baseline fluency:

Gold and XP differentials at fixed timestamps (8/10/14/20 min) and what a "normal" spread looks like this patch.
Per-role damage share, gold share, kill participation — and their role-specific baselines.
Vision metrics: wards placed/cleared per minute, control ward uptime, vision score's known weaknesses as a metric.
Jungle proximity, first-three-camps pathing, objective contest rates and steal rates (recheck all of these post-Smite-buff).
Draft data: pick/ban presence, blind-pick rates, first-rotation tendencies — and under Fearless, pool size per player per split, which is now a headline stat.

Judgement, which matters more than the metrics:

Sample size discipline. A three-week split at Bo3 gives you very little. Say so instead of over-reading it.
Base rate awareness. Champion win rates in pro play are usually noise below ~15 games.
Fearless contamination. Champion win rates are now confounded by when in a series a champion gets picked. A pick that only appears in game 4 is being played in a distorted context.
Know what the data can't see. Comms quality, scrim block health, coaching turnover, visa and travel disruption, player wellbeing, roster friction. The best analysts are explicit about the boundary between measurement and inference.
Tooling. Riot's public API, Leaguepedia/Liquipedia, GRID-derived and third-party stats platforms, and your own tracked spreadsheets. Know each source's collection method and blind spots.
6. Regional and stylistic literacy

Credible international analysis requires knowing rosters and tendencies well beyond one home region.

Per-region baselines. Every region has a characteristic pace, drafting philosophy, and risk appetite. State them as hypotheses, then check them against this season's data — regional stereotypes decay fast and lazy ones ("region X only plays late game") are how analysts lose credibility.
Roster tracking. Starters, subs, academy call-ups, and imports across all six leagues. At minimum: who plays what, and who has recently changed teams or roles.
Coach and staff mapping. Coaching staff moves predict stylistic change better than player moves do. Track head coaches, strat coaches, and analysts by name.
Tier-2 pipelines. NACL, LRN, CD, LRS, LDL, LCK CL, EMEA Masters. Guest-team slots make tier-2 directly relevant to tier-1 broadcasts.
Language and translation care. Interviews and Weibo/Naver/Reddit sourcing pass through translation. Attribute carefully; flag when you're working from a secondhand translation.
Cross-region calibration. Because domestic leagues rarely play each other, First Stand and MSI results are your only hard calibration points. Update on them, and say publicly how you're updating.
7. Historical and narrative depth

Context is expertise, not decoration.

Meta history: why previous systems were added and removed, and what happened last time something similar was tried. The 2026 objective removals are legible mainly against the 2024–25 arc.
Rule change history: how Fearless moved from LPL experiment to global standard, and how the community and pros reacted at each stage.
Team and player arcs: dynasties, rivalries, comebacks, T1's third consecutive Worlds title in 2025 and what it set up for 2026.
Format churn: the LTA's single-season existence is a useful case study in what happens when structural change outruns fan attachment.
8. Communication craft as applied expertise

Knowledge you can't deliver is not analysis.

Compression. The hardest skill on a desk is a complete, correct thought in 30 seconds between games.
Progressive disclosure. Serve the new viewer and the veteran in the same sentence: name the thing, then explain the consequence.
Falsifiable claims. Predict specifically, timestamp it, and revisit it. Audiences reward accountability more than they punish being wrong.
Register control. Broadcast, VOD review, long-form written, and short-form clips each reward a different structure. Know which you're producing.
Source hygiene. Distinguish confirmed reporting, single-source reporting, and speculation, out loud, every time.
Care with people. Roster moves, benchings, burnout and underperformance involve young professionals. Analyse decisions and performances; don't pathologise people.
